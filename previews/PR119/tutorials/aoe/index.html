A/E Pulse-Shape Parameter

Single-site events (SSE) deposit their energy in a small volume of the detector. Their current pulse is short and its maximum A is proportional to the energy E. Multi-site events (MSE), in which the energy is deposited at several places, produce a sum of pulses that arrive at different times. The maximum of the summed current is lower for the same energy. The ratio A/E therefore separates SSE from MSE. This tutorial computes A/E from simulated waveforms and compares alternative current filters. The charge-drift parameter Q-drift is computed as well.

The tutorial continues the Energy Reconstruction tutorial and uses the same steps for the baseline, the pole-zero correction and the energy, without repeating their explanation. The calibration of A/E and the cut on it are not part of LegendDSP.jl. They are done with LegendSpecFits.jl; see the documentation of that package.

Synthetic Events

The waveforms have the same properties as in the energy tutorial: a 16 ns sampling period, a decay time of 40 µs, a baseline offset, white noise and a random walk of the preamplifier output. In contrast to that tutorial, the width of the current pulse is almost the same for all events (100 ns with a spread of 2%), so that the A/E of single-site events has a narrow distribution.

An event has a current pulse that is a Gaussian of width 100 ns. For a multi-site event, a fraction f of the energy is carried by a second Gaussian pulse that is delayed by delay. Delays of tens to hundreds of nanoseconds are drawn, which are the delays between the arrival of charges deposited at different distances from the contact.

Two populations are simulated:

  • Events of the double escape peak (DEP) at 1592.5 keV (Gaussian with a standard deviation of 0.6 keV), of which 10% are multi-site.
  • Compton continuum events with a flat spectrum between 400 and 2000 keV, of which 70% are multi-site.
function simulate_aoe_waveforms(rng, energies, fractions, delays; dt = 16.0u"ns", n = 5000, t_start = 25.0u"µs",
        τ = 40.0u"µs", gain = 2f0, white_noise = 10f0, walk_noise = 0.07f0)
    time = range(0.0u"µs", step = dt, length = n)
    t = Float32.(ustrip.(u"ns", time))
    step_ns = Float32(ustrip(u"ns", dt))
    a = Float32(exp(-ustrip(NoUnits, dt / τ)))
    signal = Matrix{Float32}(undef, n, length(energies))
    pulse = similar(t)
    for (j, E) in enumerate(energies)
        t0 = Float32(ustrip(u"ns", t_start + (rand(rng) - 0.5) * 4.0u"µs"))
        width = Float32(100 * (1 + 0.02 * randn(rng)))
        f = Float32(fractions[j])
        d = Float32(ustrip(u"ns", delays[j]))
        @. pulse = ((1 - f) * exp(-0.5f0 * ((t - t0) / width)^2) + f * exp(-0.5f0 * ((t - t0 - d) / width)^2)) *
                   step_ns / (width * sqrt(2f0 * π))
        charge = cumsum(pulse)
        charge .*= gain * Float32(E)
        offset = 2000f0 + 30f0 * randn(rng, Float32)
        y = 0f0
        previous = 0f0
        for i in eachindex(t)
            y = a * y + (charge[i] - previous) + walk_noise * randn(rng, Float32)
            previous = charge[i]
            signal[i, j] = y + offset + white_noise * randn(rng, Float32)
        end
    end
    return ArrayOfRDWaveforms((fill(time, length(energies)), VectorOfSimilarVectors(signal)))
end

rng = StableRNG(7)
gain = 2.0 # ADC counts per keV
τ = 40.0u"µs"

n_dep, n_compton = 600, 6000
energies = [1592.5 .+ 0.6 .* randn(rng, n_dep); 400 .+ 1600 .* rand(rng, n_compton)]
is_dep = [trues(n_dep); falses(n_compton)]
is_mse = [rand(rng, n_dep) .< 0.1; rand(rng, n_compton) .< 0.7]
fractions = [is_mse[i] ? 0.2 + 0.6 * rand(rng) : 0.0 for i in eachindex(energies)]
delays = (50 .+ 350 .* rand(rng, length(energies))) .* u"ns"

wvfs = simulate_aoe_waveforms(rng, energies, fractions, delays; gain = Float32(gain), τ = τ)
length(wvfs)

Baseline, Pole-Zero Correction and Energy

The preprocessing is the same as in the energy tutorial. The energy is determined with a trapezoidal filter of 4 µs rise time and 2 µs flat-top time. The pick-off is taken at the center of the flat top, rise + flat / 2 after the time at which the waveform reaches 50% of its height. The energies are converted to keV with the gain of the simulation. Real data are calibrated as described in the energy tutorial.

bl = signalstats.(wvfs, 0u"µs", 20u"µs")
wvfs_pz = InvCRFilter(τ).(shift_waveform.(wvfs, -bl.mean));

e_101010 = maximum.(TrapezoidalChargeFilter(10u"µs", 10u"µs").(wvfs_pz).signal)
t50 = get_threshold(wvfs_pz, 0.5 .* e_101010; mintot = 600u"ns")
pickoff = SignalEstimator(PolynomialDNI(3, 800u"ns"))
e = pickoff.(TrapezoidalChargeFilter(4.0u"µs", 2.0u"µs").(wvfs_pz), t50 .+ 5.0u"µs") ./ gain;

Current Pulse

The current is the time derivative of the charge waveform. The derivative of the noisy waveform is dominated by noise, so it has to be smoothed. A Savitzky-Golay filter fits a polynomial of degree 3 in a window of 180 ns around each sample and gives the derivative of that polynomial. The argument 1 selects the first derivative. The result is in units of waveform samples per sample.

current_window = 15u"µs" .. 45u"µs"
wvfs_sg = SavitzkyGolayFilter(180u"ns", 3, 1).(wvfs_pz);

The maximum of the current in the window around the start of the pulse is A. get_a returns the maximum, interpolated with a parabola through the three samples around it, and its time. It is based on get_wvf_maximum.

a_sg = get_a(wvfs_sg, current_window)
keys(a_sg[1])
(:max, :t)

The current of a single-site and of a multi-site event with similar energy, with the time axis shifted to the time at which the charge reaches 50% of its height:

i_sse = findfirst(i -> !is_mse[i] && !is_dep[i] && 1400 < energies[i] < 1500, eachindex(energies))
i_mse = findfirst(i -> is_mse[i] && !is_dep[i] && 1400 < energies[i] < 1500 && delays[i] > 300u"ns" && fractions[i] > 0.4, eachindex(energies))

fig = Figure(size = (800, 380))
ax = Axis(fig[1, 1], xlabel = "Time relative to t50 (µs)", ylabel = "Current (counts / sample)", limits = (-1.5, 2.5, nothing, nothing))
for (i, label) in ((i_sse, "single-site"), (i_mse, "multi-site"))
    lines!(ax, ustrip.(u"µs", wvfs_sg[i].time) .- ustrip(u"µs", t50[i]), wvfs_sg[i].signal, label = label)

Alternative Current Filters

LegendDSP provides other filters that can be used to smooth the current:

  • WeightedSavitzkyGolayFilter fits the polynomial with weights that fall off towards the edge of the window. The weight type 2 is a Hann window. The filter returns the smoothed signal, which is differentiated afterwards with DerivativeFilter.
  • MovingWindowMultiFilter averages the derivative of the waveform in a moving window. Three passes alternating in direction are used, which makes the result symmetric.

The window lengths are of the same order, because all three filters reduce the noise with the same bandwidth.

wvfs_deriv = DerivativeFilter(1).(wvfs_pz)
wvfs_wsg = DerivativeFilter(1).(WeightedSavitzkyGolayFilter(length = 300u"ns", degree = 3, weightType = 2).(wvfs_pz))
wvfs_mwa = MovingWindowMultiFilter(100u"ns"; passes = 3).(wvfs_deriv)

a_wsg = get_a(wvfs_wsg, current_window)
a_mwa = get_a(wvfs_mwa, current_window)
a_raw = get_a(wvfs_deriv, current_window);

The filters are compared by the width of the A/E distribution of the single-site events in the DEP. The normalization is explained in the next section. A/E is divided by the median of the DEP events, most of which are single-site. The width is the standard deviation of the A/E values (in %) estimated robustly from the median absolute deviation of the DEP events above the median, which excludes the multi-site tail on the low side.

dep = findall(is_dep)
upper_width(x) = 100 * mad(x[x .> median(x)] .- median(x), center = 0, normalize = true) / median(x)

widths = map((raw = a_raw, sg = a_sg, wsg = a_wsg, mwa = a_mwa)) do a
    aoe = a.max[dep] ./ e[dep]
    upper_width(aoe)
end
(raw = 4.8792003966037445, sg = 2.3093596966569097, wsg = 2.0987320671254843, mwa = 1.7751823748878088)

The width of the unfiltered derivative is dominated by noise. The three smoothing filters give a similar, smaller width.

A/E

A/E is the ratio of the maximum of the current to the energy. Its absolute scale depends on the gain and the units of the filters. A/E is therefore normalized by the median in the DEP, which is dominated by single-site events, so that the single-site band is at 1.

aoe = a_sg.max ./ e
aoe ./= median(aoe[dep])

fig = Figure(size = (800, 380))
ax = Axis(fig[1, 1], xlabel = "A/E (normalized)", ylabel = "Events / 0.01", yscale = log10, limits = (0.4, 1.2, 0.5, nothing))
stephist!(ax, aoe, bins = 0.4:0.01:1.2, label = "all events")
stephist!(ax, aoe[dep], bins = 0.4:0.01:1.2, label = "DEP")
axislegend(ax, position = :lt)
fig

The single-site events form a peak at 1. Multi-site events are at lower values and form a tail. The tail of the DEP is the 10% of multi-site events in it. The following figure shows the energy and A/E of all events in a two-dimensional histogram.

fig = Figure(size = (800, 450))
ax = Axis(fig[1, 1], xlabel = "Energy (keV)", ylabel = "A/E (normalized)", limits = (400, 2000, 0.4, 1.2))
h2 = fit(Histogram, (e, aoe), (400:40:2000, 0.4:0.02:1.2))
counts = replace(h2.weights, 0 => NaN)
hm = heatmap!(ax, h2.edges[1], h2.edges[2], counts, colorscale = log10)
Colorbar(fig[1, 2], hm, label = "Events")
fig

The single-site events form a band at 1 and the multi-site events are spread below it. The DEP at 1592.5 keV is visible as a column of mostly single-site events.

The calibration of A/E, with a correction of its energy dependence and a cut that is applied to select single-site events, is done with LegendSpecFits.jl. For the DEP events the fraction below a cut can be read from the sample directly:

cut = 0.9
(survival_dep = mean(aoe[dep] .> cut), survival_continuum = mean(aoe[.!is_dep] .> cut))
(survival_dep = 0.9316666666666666, survival_continuum = 0.4078333333333333)

Charge Drift: Q-Drift

The charge-drift parameter Q-drift is sensitive to the arrival time of the charge. get_qdrift integrates the waveform and compares the charge collected in two successive time intervals after t0, the time at which the pulse starts: the first from t0 to t0 + first(Δt) and the second from there to t0 + last(Δt). With Δt = 2.5 µs:0.1 µs:5 µs the two intervals have the same length. For an ideal step starting at t0 both intervals contain the same charge and the difference is zero. If charge arrives later, the second interval has more charge. Q-drift is proportional to the energy and to the delay of the charge relative to t0, so Q-drift divided by the energy is a delay.

t0 = get_t0(wvfs_pz, 80; mintot = 600u"ns")
qdrift = get_qdrift(wvfs_pz, t0, 2.5u"µs":0.1u"µs":5.0u"µs");

The integrator filter used by get_qdrift sums samples, so Q-drift has the units of counts times samples. Dividing by the energy in counts and multiplying by the sampling period gives a time. For single-site events it is the time between t0 and the arrival of the charge, which is a few hundred nanoseconds because t0 is the first time the trigger filter crosses its threshold, before the charge reaches 50% of its height. For multi-site events the later charge adds to it. The two populations are separated here by the simulated truth:

step_t = step(wvfs[1].time)
qdrift_time = uconvert.(u"ns", qdrift .* step_t ./ (e .* gain))
sel = findall(.!is_dep .& (e .> 1000))
bins = 100:10:450

fig = Figure(size = (800, 380))
ax = Axis(fig[1, 1], xlabel = "Q-drift time (ns)", ylabel = "Events / 10 ns")
stephist!(ax, ustrip.(u"ns", qdrift_time[sel[.!is_mse[sel]]]), bins = bins, label = "single-site")
stephist!(ax, ustrip.(u"ns", qdrift_time[sel[is_mse[sel]]]), bins = bins, label = "multi-site")
axislegend(ax, position = :rt)
fig

The function get_lq and the filter IntegratorFilter from RadiationDetectorDSP give a related late-charge parameter, see Waveform Parameters. dsp_icpc returns A/E ingredients (a_sg, a_mwa, a_raw), qdrift and the LQ parameters for every event, as shown at the end of the Energy Reconstruction tutorial.
