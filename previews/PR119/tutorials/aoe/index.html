A/E Pulse-Shape Parameter

Single-site events (SSE) deposit their energy in a small volume of the detector. Their current pulse is short and its maximum A is proportional to the energy E. Multi-site events (MSE), in which the energy is deposited at several places, produce a sum of pulses that arrive at different times. The maximum of the summed current is lower for the same energy. The ratio A/E therefore separates SSE from MSE. This tutorial computes A/E from simulated waveforms and compares alternative current filters. The charge-drift parameter Q-drift and the late-charge parameter LQ are computed as well.

The tutorial continues the Energy Reconstruction tutorial and uses the same steps for the baseline, the pole-zero correction and the energy, without repeating their explanation. The calibration of A/E and the cut on it are not part of LegendDSP.jl. They are done with LegendSpecFits.jl; see the documentation of that package.

Synthetic Events

The waveforms have the same properties as in the energy tutorial: a 16 ns sampling period, a decay time of 40 µs, a baseline offset, white noise and a random walk of the preamplifier output. The pulse starts at (50 ± 2) µs, so that the pre-trigger baseline of at least 48 µs is longer than all filters used below. In contrast to the energy tutorial, the width of the current pulse is almost the same for all events (100 ns with a spread of 2%), so that the A/E of single-site events has a narrow distribution.

An event has a current pulse that is a Gaussian of width 100 ns. For a multi-site event, a fraction f of the energy is carried by a second Gaussian pulse that is delayed by delay. The fraction is between 15% and 50% and skewed towards small values, so that most multi-site events are just below the single-site band. 15% of the multi-site events have a third pulse with a fraction of 10% to 40%. Delays between 50 and 480 ns are drawn, so that the pulses can overlap partly. These are the delays between the arrival of charges deposited at different distances from the contact. A fraction q of the charge of an event can also arrive late, as an exponential with a time constant of 1.5 µs after the main pulse. This is used in the LQ section.

The energies follow a toy Th-228 calibration spectrum with a continuum that decreases slowly with energy and Gaussian lines with the relative intensities and the single-site fractions given in the table. The single-site fractions are typical for the full energy peaks and escape peaks of large germanium detectors: the double escape peak (DEP) consists mostly of single-site events, while most events in the single escape peak (SEP) and in the full energy peak (FEP) are multi-site.

function simulate_aoe_waveforms(rng, energies, fractions, delays, late = zeros(length(energies));
        fractions3 = zeros(length(energies)), delays3 = fill(0.0u"ns", length(energies)), dt = 16.0u"ns", n = 6000,
        t_start = 50.0u"µs", τ = 40.0u"µs", τ_late = 1.5u"µs", gain = 2f0, white_noise = 10f0, walk_noise = 0.07f0)
    time = range(0.0u"µs", step = dt, length = n)
    t = Float32.(ustrip.(u"ns", time))
    step_ns = Float32(ustrip(u"ns", dt))
    τ_late_ns = Float32(ustrip(u"ns", τ_late))
    a = Float32(exp(-ustrip(NoUnits, dt / τ)))
    signal = Matrix{Float32}(undef, n, length(energies))
    pulse = similar(t)
    for (j, E) in enumerate(energies)
        t0 = Float32(ustrip(u"ns", t_start + (rand(rng) - 0.5) * 4.0u"µs"))
        width = Float32(100 * (1 + 0.02 * randn(rng)))
        f = Float32(fractions[j])
        d = Float32(ustrip(u"ns", delays[j]))
        f3 = Float32(fractions3[j])
        d3 = Float32(ustrip(u"ns", delays3[j]))
        q = Float32(late[j])
        @. pulse = ((1 - f - f3) * exp(-0.5f0 * ((t - t0) / width)^2) + f * exp(-0.5f0 * ((t - t0 - d) / width)^2) +
                    f3 * exp(-0.5f0 * ((t - t0 - d3) / width)^2)) * step_ns / (width * sqrt(2f0 * π))
        charge = (1 - q) .* cumsum(pulse)
        @. charge += q * ifelse(t > t0, 1 - exp(-(t - t0) / τ_late_ns), 0f0)
        charge .*= gain * Float32(E)
        offset = 2000f0 + 30f0 * randn(rng, Float32)
        y = 0f0
        previous = 0f0
        for i in eachindex(t)
            y = a * y + (charge[i] - previous) + walk_noise * randn(rng, Float32)
            previous = charge[i]
            signal[i, j] = y + offset + white_noise * randn(rng, Float32)
        end
    end
    return ArrayOfRDWaveforms((fill(time, length(energies)), VectorOfSimilarVectors(signal)))
end

Baseline, Pole-Zero Correction and Energy

The preprocessing is the same as in the energy tutorial. The energy is determined with a trapezoidal filter of 4 µs rise time and 2 µs flat-top time. The pick-off is taken at the center of the flat top, rise + flat / 2 after the time at which the waveform reaches 50% of its height. The energies are converted to keV with the gain of the simulation. Real data are calibrated as described in the energy tutorial.

bl = signalstats.(wvfs, 0u"µs", 40u"µs")
wvfs_pz = InvCRFilter(τ).(shift_waveform.(wvfs, -bl.mean));

e_101010 = maximum.(TrapezoidalChargeFilter(10u"µs", 10u"µs").(wvfs_pz).signal)
t50 = get_threshold(wvfs_pz, 0.5 .* e_101010; mintot = 600u"ns")
pickoff = SignalEstimator(PolynomialDNI(3, 800u"ns"))
e = pickoff.(TrapezoidalChargeFilter(4.0u"µs", 2.0u"µs").(wvfs_pz), t50 .+ 5.0u"µs") ./ gain;

Current Pulse

The current is the time derivative of the charge waveform. The derivative of the noisy waveform is dominated by noise, so it has to be smoothed. A Savitzky-Golay filter fits a polynomial of degree 3 in a window of 180 ns around each sample and gives the derivative of that polynomial. The argument 1 selects the first derivative. The result is in units of waveform samples per sample. The window for the maximum, current_window, contains the start of the pulse.

current_window = 40u"µs" .. 70u"µs"
wvfs_sg = SavitzkyGolayFilter(180u"ns", 3, 1).(wvfs_pz);

The maximum of the current in the window around the start of the pulse is A. get_a returns the maximum, interpolated with a parabola through the three samples around it, and its time. It is based on get_wvf_maximum.

a_sg = get_a(wvfs_sg, current_window)
keys(a_sg[1])
(:max, :t)

The current of a single-site and of a multi-site event with similar energy, with the time axis shifted to the time at which the charge reaches 50% of its height:

Alternative Current Filters

LegendDSP provides other filters that can be used to smooth the current:

  • WeightedSavitzkyGolayFilter fits the polynomial with weights that fall off towards the edge of the window. The weight type 2 is a Hann window. The filter returns the smoothed signal, which is differentiated afterwards with DerivativeFilter.
  • MovingWindowMultiFilter averages the derivative of the waveform in a moving window. Three passes alternating in direction are used, which makes the result symmetric.

The window lengths are of the same order, because all three filters reduce the noise with the same bandwidth.

wvfs_deriv = DerivativeFilter(1).(wvfs_pz)
wvfs_wsg = DerivativeFilter(1).(WeightedSavitzkyGolayFilter(length = 300u"ns", degree = 3, weightType = 2).(wvfs_pz))
wvfs_mwa = MovingWindowMultiFilter(100u"ns"; passes = 3).(wvfs_deriv)

a_wsg = get_a(wvfs_wsg, current_window)
a_mwa = get_a(wvfs_mwa, current_window)
a_raw = get_a(wvfs_deriv, current_window);

The filters are compared by the width of the A/E distribution of the single-site events in the DEP. The normalization is explained in the next section. The DEP events are selected by their energy in a window of ±5 keV around the peak. A/E is divided by the median of these events, most of which are single-site. The width is the standard deviation of the A/E values (in %) estimated robustly from the median absolute deviation of the DEP events above the median, which excludes the multi-site tail on the low side.

dep = findall(abs.(e .- 1592.5) .< 5)
upper_width(x) = 100 * mad(x[x .> median(x)] .- median(x), center = 0, normalize = true) / median(x)

widths = map((raw = a_raw, sg = a_sg, wsg = a_wsg, mwa = a_mwa)) do a
    aoe = a.max[dep] ./ e[dep]
    upper_width(aoe)
end
(raw = 5.361417510210124, sg = 2.826000829051515, wsg = 2.1702078922365664, mwa = 2.012994448092165)

The width of the unfiltered derivative is dominated by noise. The three smoothing filters give a similar, smaller width.

A/E

A/E is the ratio of the maximum of the current to the energy. Its absolute scale depends on the gain and the units of the filters. A/E is therefore normalized by the median in the DEP, which is dominated by single-site events, so that the single-site band is at 1.

Charge Drift: Q-Drift

The charge-drift parameter Q-drift is sensitive to the arrival time of the charge. get_qdrift integrates the waveform and compares the charge collected in two successive time intervals after t0, the time at which the pulse starts: the first from t0 to t0 + first(Δt) and the second from there to t0 + last(Δt). With Δt = 2.5 µs:0.1 µs:5 µs the two intervals have the same length. For an ideal step starting at t0 both intervals contain the same charge and the difference is zero. If charge arrives later, the second interval has more charge. Q-drift is proportional to the energy and to the delay of the charge relative to t0, so Q-drift divided by the energy is a delay.

t0 = get_t0(wvfs_pz, 80; mintot = 600u"ns")
qdrift = get_qdrift(wvfs_pz, t0, 2.5u"µs":0.1u"µs":5.0u"µs");

The integrator filter used by get_qdrift sums samples, so Q-drift has the units of counts times samples. Dividing by the energy in counts and multiplying by the sampling period gives a time. For single-site events it is the time between t0 and the arrival of the charge, which is a few hundred nanoseconds because t0 is the first time the trigger filter crosses its threshold, before the charge reaches 50% of its height. For multi-site events the later charge adds to it. The two populations are separated here by the simulated truth:

step_t = step(wvfs[1].time)
qdrift_time = uconvert.(u"ns", qdrift .* step_t ./ (e .* gain))
sel = findall((kind .== 0) .& (e .> 1000))
bins = 100:10:600

fig = Figure(size = (800, 380))
ax = Axis(fig[1, 1], xlabel = "Q-drift time (ns)", ylabel = "Events / 10 ns")
stephist!(ax, ustrip.(u"ns", qdrift_time[sel[is_sse[sel]]]), bins = bins, label = "single-site")
stephist!(ax, ustrip.(u"ns", qdrift_time[sel[is_mse[sel]]]), bins = bins, label = "multi-site")

Late Charge: LQ

Charge that is collected late, for example charge that drifts slowly close to the surface of the detector, makes the waveform rise slowly after the main pulse. LQ is the difference of the areas under the pole-zero corrected waveform in two successive windows after the time at which the waveform reaches a fraction of its height. get_lq takes the integrated waveform, the start time, and the lengths early, gap and late of the early window, of a gap and of the late window: the early window is [t_start, t_start + early] and the late window starts after the gap. The result is the area of the late window minus the area of the early window. For a pulse with a flat top, both areas are equal and LQ is close to zero. For late charge, the waveform is still rising in the late window and LQ is positive. get_qdrift is the special case of the early window starting at t0 without a gap.

Here LQ is computed for 600 single-site events with energies between 1000 and 2600 keV, of which 30% have late charge, a fraction of 2% to 8% of the energy that arrives with a time constant of 1.5 µs.

n_lq = 600
e_lq_true = 1000 .+ 1600 .* rand(rng, n_lq)
late = [rand(rng) < 0.3 ? 0.02 + 0.06 * rand(rng) : 0.0 for _ in 1:n_lq]
wvfs_lq = simulate_aoe_waveforms(rng, e_lq_true, zeros(n_lq), fill(0.0u"ns", n_lq), late; gain = Float32(gain), τ = τ)

bl_lq = signalstats.(wvfs_lq, 0u"µs", 40u"µs")
wvfs_lq_pz = InvCRFilter(τ).(shift_waveform.(wvfs_lq, -bl_lq.mean))
e_lq = maximum.(TrapezoidalChargeFilter(10u"µs", 10u"µs").(wvfs_lq_pz).signal)
t80_lq = get_threshold(wvfs_lq_pz, 0.8 .* e_lq; mintot = 600u"ns");

The windows start at t80, the time at which the waveform reaches 80% of its height, because the rise of the charge is complete shortly after it. The windows have a length of 2.5 µs each and no gap, which is the configuration lq_windows = [[2.5u"µs", 0.0u"µs", 2.5u"µs"]] of DSPConfig used in the energy tutorial.

early, gap, late_window = 2.5u"µs", 0.0u"µs", 2.5u"µs"
lq = get_lq(IntegratorFilter(1).(wvfs_lq_pz), t80_lq, early, gap, late_window);

One event without and one with late charge, normalized to their heights. The upper panel shows the areas under the waveform in the early window (blue) and in the late window (red), which get_lq integrates. LQ is the red area minus the blue area. Both areas are close to the height times the window length, so the lower panel shows what makes them different: the area between the waveform and the flat-top level, that is the charge that has not arrived yet. LQ is the deficit of the early window minus the deficit of the late window. It is larger for the event with late charge, because most of its deficit is in the early window and much less is left in the late window.

i_ss = findfirst(i -> late[i] == 0 && 1500 < e_lq_true[i] < 1900, 1:n_lq)

LQ is proportional to the energy. Dividing it by the energy in counts and multiplying with the sampling period gives a time, as for Q-drift. The two populations are separated in LQ over the energy range:

has_late = late .> 0

Events without late charge scatter around a small value that is given by the shape of the rise. The late-charge events are spread to larger values, according to the simulated fraction of late charge. In an analysis, a cut is set on the energy-normalized LQ, as for A/E. dsp_icpc returns LQ for every window of config.lq_windows and for the start times t80, t90 and t_amax, in the columns lq80_<early><gap><late>, lq90_<early><gap><late> and lqamax_<early><gap><late>. The table of the energy tutorial contains, for example, the column lq80_250025.
